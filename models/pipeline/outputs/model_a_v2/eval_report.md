## In plain words

- **What Model A does.** For every place and week it predicts how many dengue cases should be reported, from earlier weeks only, and flags weeks that came in far too low. A flag is a lead for staff to check, not a confirmed error.
- **What changed in v2.** (1) It follows the recent level of reporting, so it adjusts after a real drop such as COVID-19 in 2020 instead of flagging it for months. (2) Its 90% ranges also account for uncertainty in the model itself and for some years being bigger epidemics than others. (3) Its threshold was set on 2019 using simulated failures. (4) Every week now carries `p_fail`, a probability that the report is a real failure, and `shortfall`, the cases likely missing. (5) Region-wide evidence is pooled allowing for places that move together.
- **Alert budget 3% (run 3).** The share of normal weeks Model A may flag was raised from 1% to 3% at the team's request, chosen on 2016–2019 only (design log in config.py). It catches more real errors for more false alerts; the 2020 numbers below are a second look at 2020, not an independent test (see the test-run log).
- **False alarms in 2020** (normal weeks wrongly flagged): v1 22.6% → v2 10.1% (budget 3%).
- **Real swapped reports caught in 2020:** v1 77%, v2 32%, best simple baseline 8%. Simulated failures caught in 2020: v1 53%, v2 39%.
- **Region-wide alerts in 2020:** false region alerts went from 71% (v1 as reported) to 15% (v2 with correlation-adjusted pooling), while 58% of the real swap region-weeks are still flagged. Most of that drop comes from giving region alerts their own threshold (v1 with its own region threshold: 36%); v2's model and pooling take it the rest of the way.
- **Roadmap targets met: 2 of 4** (table below). Targets that were missed are reported as missed; nothing was re-tuned on 2020.
- **Bottom line.** v2 is not better than v1 on every count; it is a different trade-off. It cuts false alarms (individual weeks 23% → 10%, regions 71% → 15%) and ranks simulated failures better (AUC 0.77 → 0.85), but it catches far fewer of the real 2020 swaps, because once it has followed the COVID-era low counts a zero no longer looks unusual. Neither version holds the 3% budget through the April–June 2020 drop: a level change that sudden and that large is outside anything in 2016–2019. Use v2's region (hub) evidence for shared failures; treat single-week alerts in a period of sudden real change with caution.

## How the test was kept honest

- **Split:** train 2016–2018 (history only), tune 2019, test 2020. Scores are rolling-origin: each week is predicted from earlier weeks only (`tests/test_no_leakage.py` checks this).
- **Model choice before the test:** 10 variants were compared on 2016–2019 only, with a rule fixed in advance (90% range coverage 85–95% on 2017–2019, then the best 2019 recall on injected failures; since run 3 the score also includes recall on the real 2016–2017 swaps, at the 3% budget). Chosen: **`recent1+uncertainty`**: coverage 0.859 is inside [0.85, 0.95] and it has the best the mean of 2019 injected-failure recall and 2016-2017 real swap recall (0.610) among those that are. The choice was written to `selection.json` before any 2020 metric was computed.
- **Same failures for both versions:** v1's injected failures are re-read from `models/data/synthetic/model_a_injected_failures.csv`, not re-drawn.
- **Faithful port:** v1 re-run inside this package reproduces the published v1 `scores.csv` (`tests/test_v1_reproduces.py`).

### Test-run log

The 2020 test year has been scored 3 times. **Run 1** had an implementation bug in the threshold rule: the 1% false-alert cap was counted on the evaluation copy's normal weeks, whose injected failures widen the model, instead of on the real 2019 stream. ## Shared-source evidence (region = hub)

Real data has no reporting-hub table, so each region's RESU is used as the hub (`hub_id = RESU-<region>`). Fisher pooling (v1) treats the places in a region as independent witnesses; correlation-adjusted Stouffer (v2) discounts them by their average correlation on normal 2016–2018 weeks. Every (model, pooling) pair gets its own region-week threshold at 3% of clean 2019 region-weeks; the first row repeats v1's own report (at its 1%).

## Limits

- Flags are **leads to verify**, not confirmed errors. Real labels are the 581 swapped rows and 3 regional gaps found during cleaning; other failures in the data are unknown and count as normal weeks here.
- Injected failures are simulated. `p_fail` is calibrated on dengue province-weeks; for other diseases it is reported but not validated.
- The hub is the region's RESU because real reporting pathways are not available. The synthetic reporting network (roadmap step 3) tests real hub structure.
- The year-to-year term needs at least 26 reported periods in a year, so it is inactive for the monthly TB series.
- 2019 was a national dengue epidemic year; thresholds tuned on it may be conservative in calmer years.
